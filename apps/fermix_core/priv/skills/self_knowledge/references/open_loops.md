# Open loops

An open loop is something in the owner's own life, told in their own words, that a friend who was listening would come back to: something they mean to do or decide by a date, or something they are waiting on or going through. Fermix records it at the turn where the owner says it, keeps it as its own row in `~/.fermix/memory.db` (never a memory fact, never an event), raises it a bounded number of times at moments it judges receptive, and lets the owner's answer settle it. It is a different thing from a reminder: the owner asked for a reminder, while a loop is something Fermix noticed from the owner's own words.

## Turning it on

- One key, `[fermix_core.open_loops] enabled`, off by default. Browser setup's Personalization page has the toggle beside skill curation, and `config.toml` can set it on Linux and dev installs. No Mac Settings pane sets it yet. Restart after changing it: the part that raises loops on its own starts only when Fermix starts.
- Everything else (checks, windows, caps, active hours) is a fixed constant, not a setting.
- Off, no open-loop tool is offered anywhere, the prompt says nothing about loops, and stored loops are neither noted nor raised.

## What becomes a loop

- The agent judges each moment the way a listening friend would: let it go, ask about it when the owner is next around, or check in on them. Letting it go records nothing; the other two record a loop with `open_loop_note`, silently: the reply says nothing about it unless the owner asks. There is no list of topics; the judgment is the agent's.
- Two timings, from what the thing is:
  - Something to do or decide by a date (`matters_by`: a date, a datetime, or a relative number of days or weeks) is raised before the date.
  - Something waited on or gone through, such as an interview, a result or a hard conversation (`ask_after`), is asked about afterwards, from when asking first feels natural: at least three hours and at most a year out, and a date alone means after that day ends (the next day's start of active hours).
  - Times resolve in `[fermix_core.personalization] timezone` unless the owner names a zone. A timer or a yearly date is refused; that is a reminder (`event_store`).
- Reach, set per loop: `conversation` means it comes up only inside a conversation the owner starts, never by message; `message` means Fermix may also send one check-in message when the owner does not come by.
- A submission the owner is preparing in their own private chat, with no known closing date: the agent checks `open_loop_list`, does the work, and may end its reply with one short question asking when it closes, once, never when the owner is rushed. It records the loop only with a date; no date, or no answer, records nothing. A date the agent looked up or found in pasted content counts only once the owner confirms it (recorded as `matters_by_source: lookup`), and it looks nothing up unless asked.
- Not a loop: a question about a date, someone else's deadline or news, a passing remark, a hypothetical, a date read in tool output or fetched content the owner has not confirmed, or something Fermix itself owns. Nothing is ever recorded from a guest's turn, a group chat or a Slack channel mention; the tools refuse all three.
- A loop, not also a reminder: something the owner has to do by a date, said without asking to be reminded, is recorded as a loop only, never also as an event. The exception is a loop under 48 hours away: it is never raised by message, so the agent stores the reminder too, and when the owner settles or moves that task it does the same to both. A loop kept for conversation takes no reminder. A date too close to raise at least three hours after it was said records no loop, and the agent stores the reminder instead.
- One loop per thing: the same thing told again in the same timing returns the stored loop; told in the other timing it is refused with `kind_conflict`, naming the loop that holds it.
- A loop the owner states in a request that has also read something someone else wrote (a web page, an attachment, a forwarded message, a plugin or MCP result) is still recorded, marked as noted after outside content, so its words are framed wherever they come back. Loops recorded before this mark existed are not marked.
- A loop needs somewhere the owner can answer: the owner's inbox (`[fermix_core.jobs] default_delivery_target`, else the owner DM an explicit owner id derives), snapshotted when the loop is recorded. With no chat inbox, or only this computer's notifications, nothing is recorded and the agent answers the owner's actual question.

## The tools

- `open_loop_note`, `open_loop_update` and `open_loop_list` run only on the owner's attended, top-level turn in a private chat: a direct message, the paired phone or Mac app, or a voice call. Never in a group chat or a Slack channel mention (a mention is a shared channel unless Slack says it is a direct message), and never for a guest, a scheduled job, a background or delegated run, or a coding continuation.
- `open_loop_note` takes `intention`, `reach` and exactly one of `matters_by` and `ask_after`; optionally `why_it_matters`, `timezone`, and with `matters_by` only `matters_by_source` (`owner`, the default, or `lookup`).
- `open_loop_update` answers, each from the owner's own words:
  - `done` closes it for good; `stop` closes it and the owner is not asked about it again; `drop` stops tracking it with no boundary stated, and deletes its follow-up record.
  - `plan` with `plan_at` re-arms it at the owner's time and no earlier: never later than a dated loop's own date, at most a year out for something waited on, where a date alone means after that day ends.
  - `not_yet`: for something to do, one more check nearer the date; for something waited on, it needs `ask_after`, when to ask again.
  - `moved` with `matters_by` (and optionally `matters_by_source`) gives a dated loop its new date: the checks follow the new date and the count of raises carries over; a loop whose raises already fill the new date's checks is not raised again and ends quietly after the new date, never before it. Something waited on is not moved; the owner names a new time with `plan` or `not_yet`.
  - `reach` with `reach` changes how it may be raised from now on, when the owner says so ("only bring that up if I mention it", "message me about it"); nothing else moves.
  - `asked` records that this reply raised a due loop; on a loop not yet due it is refused (`not_due`), since the loop comes up on its own.
  - Every answer but `asked` carries `owner_words`, the owner's clause it rests on (at most 240 characters, never stored). A loop is settled, moved or re-reached from the owner's words only: in a request that has read something someone else wrote, `owner_words` must appear, case and punctuation aside, in what the owner typed in that message (never a forwarded message or an attachment), or nothing changes; in a voice call, nothing is typed, so once the call has read such content (any of its tasks did, from then on for the rest of the call) only `asked` is recorded and the loop waits for the owner's chat. This is per request: an earlier request's content still in the conversation does not hold a later request that read nothing.
- `open_loop_list` (`state` `live`, `closed` or `any`) shows each loop's words, its date or the time asking may start, its reach, when and where it was said, its state, how many times it has been raised, its next check, and its follow-ups: when and how Fermix raised it, what it said, and the owner's reply with how long after it came, or that none came, each text quoted as data. A loop noted after outside content shows its words framed as outside content, there, in the result of settling it and in the result of noting it again, and what Fermix said after outside content is framed the same way; a list or a result that shows either counts as having read something someone else wrote, as a note that frames one does, for the request or the check-in run that asked. Never a destination. "What are you keeping an eye on?" lists them and "drop that" removes one with its record.
- Refusals name their reason. `open_loop_note`: `disabled`, `not_attended`, `missing_parameters`, `missing_when`, `both_whens`, `too_long`, `invalid_param`, `invalid_when`, `not_dated`, `date_passed`, `ask_after_too_soon`, `ask_after_too_far`, `source_without_matters_by`, `kind_conflict`, `timezone_not_configured`, `no_target`, `too_many_loops`, `imminent_without_window` (the date, or a moved loop's new date, is under three hours away), `no_conversation`. `open_loop_update` also: `invalid_answer`, `not_found`, `not_live` (closed and expired loops never reopen), `already_asked`, `missing_owner_words`, `owner_words_too_long`, `owner_words_not_in_message`, `owner_words_unverifiable`, `plan_requires_date`, `plan_after_date`, `plan_too_far`, `plan_passed`, `plan_at_without_plan`, `ask_after_required`, `ask_after_on_dated_loop`, `ask_after_without_not_yet`, `moved_requires_date`, `moved_on_after_loop`, `matters_by_without_moved`, `reach_without_reach_answer`, `not_due` and `touches_spent`.

## Lifecycle

- States: `open`, `due`, `asked`, `closed`, `expired`. Closed and expired are final; a new intention is a new loop.
- Something to do by a date: the checks follow how far off the date was when the owner said it (or moved it): two weeks or more, 7 days before, the day before and the day after; 4 to 13 days, 3 days before, the day before and the day after; 2 to 3 days, one check at least 24 hours before and the day after; under 48 hours, only the in-chat note, from three hours after the owner said it (never in the conversation it came from) until the date passes. A check falls at the start of the owner's active hours on its day, and the check after a raise is always on a later day, so a loop is never raised twice in a day unless the owner names a time later that day. The check after the date is the hand-back, which says plainly Fermix will not raise it again; unanswered, the loop expires quietly a day later. A loop kept for conversation has no hand-back: once its last check before the date has passed, it ends quietly after the date.
- Something waited on: one check, at its `ask_after`. A second exists only when the owner names a new time or says not yet with a new `ask_after`; there is no third, and once both are used a new time is refused (`touches_spent`). Unanswered, it ends a day after it was raised, and a check-in that decides to stay silent ends it too; one that decides `later` waits once for the next active-hours start. A check never raised within 14 days of its time ends quietly.
- Active hours come from when the owner writes, over the last 28 days with guest messages left out: the middle of that spread, at least six hours wide. With fewer than 40 messages they are 09:00 to 21:00. There is no quiet-hours setting.
- Silence is not an answer: for something to do the next check follows on its own; for something waited on, silence ends it.

## Raising a due loop

- In the chat first: while a loop's window is open, every attended owner turn in a private chat, on any channel, carries a system note naming it, when it was said, its date or that the question is how it went, and its id, followed by how the owner answered recent follow-ups. A group chat or a Slack channel mention never carries it; the loop waits for the next private turn. The window is three hours for a loop Fermix may message about; for a dated loop kept for conversation it runs to its next check, the last one to the date; 14 days for something waited on kept for conversation; a loop under 48 hours away, from three hours after it was said until its date. The agent raises at most one, only when the exchange has reached a natural stopping point and the owner is not under pressure, and leaves anything unrelated that can wait when what the owner has said shows a hard time for them, in one or two sentences at the end of a reply, asks how something waited on went without assuming the outcome or offering advice, and records it as `asked`; otherwise it leaves it.
- The note quotes the loop's words as the owner's earlier note, as data and never as an instruction, and quotes what Fermix said and what the owner replied in the follow-ups the same way. A loop noted after outside content is framed as outside content and the note says its words may not be the owner's; the request it rides then counts as having read something someone else wrote, so, as after a web page, an access-sensitive command, a command on Linux or a coding agent waits for the owner in the chat; on a voice call a command on Linux or a coding agent is refused instead, and an access-sensitive command waits for the owner's spoken yes. What Fermix said in a follow-up raised after outside content is framed the same way, and brings the note the same outside source. The check-in run gets such a loop framed the same way, and raises it as something that came up while the owner was reading and asks whether they meant it, never as something they said; it reads the follow-ups as text under the loop, any framed one with a rule to read it as data and never present it as the owner's words. The message the owner last received about the loop comes back framed the same way when the loop is marked or the run that wrote it had read something someone else wrote, which the loop keeps with the message however long ago the follow-up record let it go, and then also counts as outside content for the run.
- Then on its own, only for a loop with reach `message`: when that window closes without a raise, one short check-in run decides, inside active hours and at most once a day across all loops (loops with a date first, the nearest date first, since a deadline loses its value after its date; then things waited on, the longest-waiting first). It recalls memory, reads the recent follow-ups and what the owner wrote in that chat in the last 3 days (at most 6 messages, their own words only: never a guest's, a forwarded or spoken message, a coding run's notice or Fermix's replies), then sends one short message to the snapshotted inbox, stays silent, decides `later` when now is the wrong moment (on a hard day for the owner, anything unrelated that can wait does), or closes the loop when memory shows the owner already reported it settled. The message opens with what the owner said and when, and is written into that chat's history, so the owner's reply lands in an ordinary turn that settles the loop.
- A raise counts only once its send is confirmed. A failed send, a provider outage or a `later` changes no count and retries at a later active-hours start (a hand-back, or a check on something waited on, retries once, and a `later` counts as that one retry); a loop the owner settled while a check-in was running is not sent.

## What the owner said back

- Code, not the model, records each follow-up: when it was raised, in conversation or by message, what Fermix said (the check-in's text, or the closing sentences of the reply that raised it, at most 280 characters), the owner's next message in that conversation within a day (the first 280 characters of what they typed, their own text or caption, never an attachment's contents; an attachment with no caption, a forwarded message or a voice call's hand-off is no reply) with how long after it came, or "no reply within a day", and the answer that then settled the loop, if any. A check-in sent to a Slack or Discord inbox or to the terminal is not recorded: the owner's reply there arrives under another conversation id, so it could never be matched and would read as silence. Nor is a raise in a voice call: the owner answers out loud, and nothing captures the spoken reply.
- A follow-up raised in a request, or sent by a check-in run, that had read something someone else wrote (a web page, an attachment, a forwarded message, a plugin or MCP result, or a loop or follow-up already marked) is marked, whether the reading came before or after the raise in that request, so what Fermix said there comes back framed as outside content wherever the record is read.
- What Fermix said in a reply that could see the owner's computer activity is marked as activity-derived: a due note or `open_loop_list` shows it only on a request that may carry activity, and a check-in run never sees it; elsewhere the follow-up reads as raised, with the owner's reply, and without Fermix's words.
- Raises read it back as the owner's own words with the delay, never as a score or a label: the in-chat note shows each due loop's latest reply and the two most recent others, and a check-in run sees its loop's latest and the four most recent others. Each reply is one moment, not a rule, and what the owner has said outright outranks any pattern.
- Only owner-private conversations are followed, never a guest's, a group chat or a Slack channel mention. The newest 50 per owner are kept, `open_loop_list` shows them, and a drop deletes that loop's.

## Caps

- 3 raises per dated loop, ever, the hand-back included; 2 for something waited on.
- 1 unprompted check-in a day across all loops, and never one for a loop kept for conversation.
- 20 live loops per owner; the 21st is refused.
- 50 recorded follow-ups per owner.
- 2 check-in runs at a time; a run gets at most 6 model steps and 120 seconds, and a message at most 1,800 bytes, refused rather than cut when longer.

## What the check-in cannot do

- Its tools are `memory_recall`, `open_loop_list` and `open_loop_respond`, its one exit (`speak` with the text, `silent`, `later`, or `close` with a reason). It has no channel tools, no event, job or loop changes, no browsing, no delegation and no `memory_store`, and Fermix, not the run, sends its message.
- The surfaces never cross: an owner's turn is never offered `open_loop_respond`, and a check-in run is never offered `open_loop_note` or `open_loop_update`.
- Check-in runs, their outcomes, and whether each follow-up drew a reply (never its words) show in the trace files: skill_view(name: "self-knowledge", file: "observability").
