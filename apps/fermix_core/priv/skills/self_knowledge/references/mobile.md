# Mobile companion (phone channel)

An owner-only channel from the owner's phone to their own Fermix daemon. It is not a hosted chat service: messages stay between the phone and the host, and a paired device enters the gateway as the operator. Fermix owns the versioned wire contract; the phone apps live in their own repositories.

**No phone app is released.** The Android app is in development and the iPhone app has not shipped. What exists is the daemon half, so there is nothing to install or scan yet. Tell an owner who asks exactly that, point them at the chat channels that work today, and never walk them through installing an app or pairing.

## Turning it on

On by default: an absent `[fermix_channels.mobile]` section, or one without `enabled`, means enabled, and `enabled = false` keeps it off. An install that saved its settings while the channel was off by default carries that `enabled = false` line, so it stays off until the line changes. The daemon publishes two management surfaces:

- the `channels.mobile` settings section: `enabled`, `iroh` (the "Reach from anywhere" row, see *Remote reach*), `port`, `bind` (`0.0.0.0` for every network, or one literal IP such as the Tailscale address; anything else is refused by name) and `advertise_mdns` (the "Announce on the local network" row, off by default). A write lands in `config.toml` at once. `enabled` is live: turning it on starts the channel and turning it off stops it, with no restart (turning it off drops connected phones and closes an open pairing code; paired phones stay paired). `iroh` is live too, within a running channel. `port`, `bind` and `advertise_mdns` are read when the channel starts, so a change to them takes a restart. Every channel, the phone among them, starts only once setup is finished: a daemon that started before setup was done (no working AI provider yet, for one) holds the phone channel, `mobile.status` reads `hold` `setup`, and pairing says "The phone channel starts once setup is finished." A restart before setup is finished holds it again, so finish setup first; once it is, and while the channel is on, the restart reasons carry a `channels` reason and the next restart starts it.
- the `mobile.*` methods: `mobile.status`, the pairing session (`mobile.pair.start`, `mobile.pair.get`, `mobile.pair.decide`, `mobile.pair.cancel`) and paired devices (`mobile.devices.list`, `mobile.devices.revoke`, `mobile.devices.set_executor`).

The Mac app's and the Linux app's Phone rows in their Channels settings, and the browser setup's Phone card on its Channels tab, are built on them: each turns the channel on and pairs a phone in one place (a code to scan, the six digits to compare, **Approve** or **Deny**), lists the paired phones with **Forget…**, and carries Reach from anywhere and the advanced rows. On an app-managed Mac, enabling and pairing belong to the app, never the CLI. On a dev or Linux host, `fermix pair` finds a channel that is off, asks, turns it on and pairs, in one command. To turn it on without pairing, run `fermix settings set channels.mobile mobile_enabled=true`, or set

```toml
[fermix_channels.mobile]
enabled = true
```

in `config.toml` and run `fermix settings reload`; neither needs a restart. The terminal wizard has no mobile step or flag.

**Only the owner pairs or forgets a phone.** Starting, deciding or cancelling a pairing and revoking a phone come from the apps, the owner's browser setup session, or a command the daemon places as typed in the owner's terminal. A command Fermix started itself (an agent's shell command, a coding run, a scheduled job), a detached one, or one it cannot place is refused with "Only the owner can pair or forget a phone; run this from your own terminal." So never run `fermix pair` or `fermix devices revoke` through `shell`; tell the owner to. Reading the status, the session and the phone list stays open.

Hand-edit only: `streaming` (draft streaming is the default) and `max_media_bytes`. `media_store_max_bytes` sets the media retention budget (2 GiB by default).

## Reachability and security

- The phone connects to a dedicated listener, `wss://HOST:4031/ws` by default (`/healthz` is its only other route), over the LAN or any shared network such as a Tailscale tailnet. Binding to `0.0.0.0` exposes only this listener; setup and the web UI stay loopback-only. On a fresh install the listener stays dormant until the first pairing starts, which creates this host's phone identity; from then on it starts at every boot. It serves only paired phones.
- The phone learns where to connect from the pairing link and from each reconnect's `hello_ack`: this host's private-LAN and tailnet addresses (with a tailnet address's MagicDNS name), up to 16, best first. A `bind` of one address lists only that address and its MagicDNS name, because the listener answers nowhere else. If the host cannot list its addresses, a reconnect still succeeds with none, and the phone keeps the ones it holds.
- The local-network announcement (`_fermix._tcp` over mDNS, also called Bonjour) is off by default, and the Android app does not use it. Turn it on with `advertise_mdns = true` in `[fermix_channels.mobile]` or the "Announce on the local network" row, then restart.
- TLS satisfies the phone platform; trust comes from an end-to-end Noise session between device and gateway keys. The pairing QR carries the pinned certificate fingerprint and gateway public key. No bearer token, port forwarding or public funnel is involved, and the LAN and tailnet routes use no relay.
- The listener bounds a peer that never finishes: 64 connections at once, 10 seconds for TLS, 15 seconds to the WebSocket upgrade, 10 seconds for the Noise hello, 64 KiB per client frame. An app built for a protocol version the daemon does not speak is refused with an error naming which side must update.
- A listener that cannot bind (`address_unavailable`, `address_in_use`, `permission_denied`) never stops the daemon: `mobile.status` reads it `unavailable` with that reason, and it retries (one second doubling to a minute) for up to a day, then waits for the next restart.
- Host material lives in `$FERMIX_HOME/mobile/` (`0700`): `gateway_key`, `tls.crt`, `tls.key`, `iroh_key`, `devices.toml` and the `media/` store; keys and the device list must stay `0600`.

## Remote reach

- "Reach from anywhere" (`iroh = true` in `[fermix_channels.mobile]`, on by default; `iroh = false` or the row turns it off at once, no restart) lets a paired phone reach this host from any network, with no Tailscale and no account, through Fermix's own relays. The daemon runs a small helper, `fermix-iroh` (an iroh endpoint with key `mobile/iroh_key`), and gives the phone its route in the pairing code and at each reconnect. The phone's session inside is the same end-to-end Noise session, so a relay sees addresses, sizes and timing, never messages.
- **Release builds carry the helper.** The formula binaries, the Mac app's engine and the Linux packages each include the pinned `fermix-iroh`, so the switch needs no download and no extra step. A source checkout has none until its owner runs `mix fermix.iroh_helper` and restarts the daemon; until then `mobile.status` reads remote reach `helper_missing`, `fermix doctor` warns and names that task, and a phone reaches Fermix over the LAN or Tailscale only. A release that reads `helper_missing` is broken: Doctor fails it and names that install's way back (reinstall Fermix.app, `brew reinstall fermix`, or the package manager's reinstall).
- `bind` and the local-network announcement never limit remote reach: a host listening only on its Tailscale address is still reachable through the relays while "Reach from anywhere" is on. Turning it off is the one way to keep a phone to the home network and Tailscale. The settings keep `port`, `bind` and `advertise_mdns` under Advanced (their rows carry `advanced`).
- On a host with no phone paired yet it stays `dormant`: relay contact starts with the first pairing window, and stays on after it even if that pairing is abandoned.
- A helper that fails never stops the daemon or the phone channel: remote reach reads `failed` with the helper's words, retries (one second doubling to a minute) for up to a day, and LAN and Tailscale keep working. A helper built for another protocol, iroh version or key fails without retrying.
- When a release adds a relay, the daemon uses it only once every paired phone has reconnected and named the new list (`hello.relay_list_rev`), so a phone that was away still reaches a relay it knows.
- `mobile.status.remote` publishes the state (`off`, `dormant`, `helper_missing`, `starting`, `ready`, `failed`), the endpoint id and relay while ready, whether the relay is connected, and the remote connections refused (`busy`, `alpn`, `bridge_unreachable`).

## Pairing

- `mobile.pair.start` opens the one pairing window (120 seconds; a second start while it is open is refused as busy) and returns the pairing link once, on that call only, with up to 16 host addresses, best first; the link's single-use secret is never logged or repeated and expires with the window. A refused start answers `failed` with the daemon's sentence: the channel is off, could not start this boot, waits for setup to be finished, was turned on but not yet restarted, identity files are incomplete, the device list is unreadable, or the listener could not start.
- Session `state`: `awaiting_scan` (window open, `ttl_ms` left); `awaiting_decision` (a phone completed the Noise handshake and its secure-hardware proof was verified; `request` carries its name, model, platform, app version, the six-digit SAS, its build (`release` or `development`) and the attestation line "Secure hardware, locked bootloader, official Fermix build."); terminal `approved` (with the new device id), `denied`, `expired` (`timeout`) or `cancelled`; or `failed`, with nothing saved: `device_disconnected` before a decision, or the phone's proof was refused or Google's attestation service could not be reached, in which case the owner was never asked and the sentence names why. A finished session stays readable for a few minutes (at most eight kept).
- A phone must prove its secure hardware to pair, so an emulator, a phone with an unlocked bootloader or an unverified system, or an app not signed with Fermix's own keys cannot pair (the checks are under *Attestation* in the mobile protocol, `priv/mobile/PROTOCOL.md`). Already paired phones keep working.
- Failed handshakes never close the window: an address that fails five times is refused for the rest of that window and no other address is, and nothing is counted while a phone waits for the owner's decision. The same phone asking again replaces its own waiting request.
- `fermix pair` drives this session: it prints the QR, time left and manual link, polls every second, shows the attestation line and the device with its build, boot state and SAS, and approves only on an explicit `y` (blank or closed input denies). Compare the SAS on phone and terminal first. It cancels the window if anything fails. Approval stores the device key in `devices.toml`. Device names and models over 128 bytes or with control characters are refused before the owner is asked.
- `fermix devices list` (`mobile.devices.list`): paired ids, names, created and last-seen times, oldest first; with the channel off it reads `devices.toml`. `fermix devices revoke DEVICE_ID` (`mobile.devices.revoke`) removes the key, drops its live socket at once, and cancels every unfinished request from that phone so none runs again at the next boot; with the channel off it edits `devices.toml` the same way. An unknown id answers "No paired phone has that id." Unpairing on the phone requests removal, but host revocation is authoritative for a lost phone.

- `pair_approved` and every `hello_ack` carry the home's primary epoch; the phone keeps the highest it has seen and refuses a lower one as a stale primary, and Fermix refuses a phone that has seen a higher epoch with `stale_primary`, because that phone has already met the engine this home moved to (see the `home` reference).

## Desktops (Always On)

After a move (see the `home` reference) the desktop the home left becomes a paired device of the new primary, of kind `desktop`: its row in `devices.toml` has `kind = "desktop"` and an `executor` switch, and no push or attestation field (a row that names no kind is a phone's). It connects like a phone, with its own key, and the primary admits only a desktop's events from it: its app's chat tunneled to the primary's companion socket, forwarded management calls, the results of actions the primary asks it to run, sign-in loopbacks and snapshot copies. It sends no phone chat and is sent no phone notification or approval card. `mobile.devices.list` shows each device's kind and a desktop's switch (`fermix devices list` prints ids, names and times only), and `mobile.status`'s paired-device count is the phones', never a desktop. `mobile.devices.set_executor` turns the switch (the owner's decision, like forgetting a device), and a desktop runs an action only when both this switch and its own `[fermix_core.engine] executor` allow it (what it runs, its approval cards and its receipts: the `home` reference). `fermix devices revoke` and the Mac or Linux app forget a desktop like a phone; a revoked desktop stops dialing and says the server no longer knows it. A desktop's event outside the families the primary serves is refused by name (`unsupported_event`) and the session stays up, a forward-compatibility rule.

## Chat behavior

- All paired phones share the `main` conversation, and the Mac app's chat reads the same timeline, so reconnects, a second phone and the Mac see the same history; every row is announced live to each connected device. The phone queues offline messages and resends them by client message id; the host deduplicates and resynchronizes with a monotonic cursor. Read state is shared and never passes the newest row.
- The phone's turns run in the Mac app's chat conversation: one agent history for the one timeline. A turn from the phone reads what was said on the Mac (a GPT-Live call's tasks in the chat included) and the reverse, and a phone message waits for a running Mac turn or voice task and the reverse. `/new` from the phone starts that one conversation afresh for both. The channel is still the phone's: pairing, approval cards, streaming and push are unchanged.
- During a GPT-Live call in the chat, a message typed on the phone is passed to the voice like one typed on the Mac, its answer too, and its turn knows the call is up; it always answers in writing, because the phone's channel writes each reply as it comes and cannot end a turn silently.
- Text, tool activity, photos and documents both ways (inbound, any attachment the pipeline reads; a GIF or video arrives as its frames when ffmpeg is installed on the host, else as the poster frame the phone sends with it), voice notes, slash commands and the command palette, link previews (stored with their row, each with the site's icon beside the page's picture), reactions, and approve/deny cards (including an access-sensitive plugin command, run once approved). Voice notes use the configured transcription backend; this is not realtime voice, and phone calls are not supported. A recording the app marks as a voice note is the owner's own words; an audio file attached to a message counts as content someone else could have written, so a marked command asked for beside one waits for the owner's confirmation (plugins reference).
- A reply streams into a live draft only on a streaming provider route (Codex today); otherwise it arrives whole when the turn ends.
- An event over one frame arrives in parts, up to 1 MiB; a longer reply is cut there and marked truncated on the phone while the stored row keeps it whole. A history page holds at most 256 KiB and its cursor pulls the rest.
- The phone's Stop cancels one request by its client message id (running, waiting or not yet queued), never a Mac turn in the queue they share; `/stop` still stops everything.
- Reactions reach only a connected phone; they are not stored.
- An approval card goes only to the chat whose turn raised it (a phone's never shows on the Mac, and the reverse). A card still waiting is sent again when a phone reconnects, with the time it has left.
- A failed message or command comes back by its client message id with its own code (`store_quota_exceeded`, `media_too_large`, `attachment_unavailable`) or `request_failed`.
- Uploads are capped by `max_media_bytes` (20 MiB by default), four in flight per phone and sixteen in all, and never evict stored media; an attach id is valid for two days. The phone strips image location data; the host keeps content-addressed media under `mobile/media/` so another phone can fetch attachments from history.

- A restart answers a message Fermix had not started; one in progress, or anything unfinished after the home moved or was restored, ends as `uncertain_after_restart` (`request_status` reports `failed` with that word), and the owner sends it again as a new message; the original id is a duplicate.

## Push (APNs)

When no device socket is connected and no other device has read the new content, Fermix sends one direct APNs notification per registered iPhone; scheduled and background deliveries follow the same rule, whether they reach the shared chat through the phone's channel or the Mac's (so `schedule_job` with `delivery_mode: "origin"` from the phone, which delivers into the shared chat as the Mac chat does, still pushes a phone that is away, and an owner reading on the Mac is not pinged). An approval raised on the phone while no device is connected pushes a content-free "Approval needed". The payload carries a per-device encrypted preview, never plaintext, but no phone can decrypt it yet, so the alert reads only "New message". Configure `[fermix_channels.mobile.push]` by hand: `enabled`, `team_id`, `key_id`, `topic`, `environment` (`development` or `production`). The `.p8` key follows the normal secret path: `FERMIX_APNS_KEY`, or write it into `config.toml` once and move it to the keychain with `fermix setup --migrate-secrets`; never leave it in plain TOML. Fermix connects to Apple on the first push, never at boot; while connecting or unreachable, push reads `degraded` and the daemon keeps running.

An Android phone gets no push: Fermix has no push service for Android, so it never lists one to the phone (`caps.push`, where the app looks before offering notifications). A token an Android phone registered anyway is kept and never sent to APNs, and each push it would have had is logged as refused for that phone. An older `[fermix_channels.mobile.fcm]` table in `config.toml` is ignored with a warning at every start, and the next settings save writes the file without it; its keychain item is never read.

## Troubleshooting

`fermix doctor` checks gateway keys and TLS files (`0600`), listener reachability on advertised addresses, mDNS, tailnet detection, remote reach ("reach from anywhere ready via <relay>", off, waiting for the first pairing, unavailable without the helper, not connected to a relay, or failed in the helper's words), APNs credentials and delivery, and the paired-device count. An enabled channel with no phone paired passes as "on, no phone paired yet"; an incomplete identity or wrong permissions fails, because Fermix refuses such files rather than regenerating them. "mobile surface refused this boot; see the daemon log" and "mobile channel not started; restart the daemon" replace the per-probe rows; a listener that cannot bind reads "listener unavailable (reason); it keeps retrying". `mobile.status` publishes the same facts (`enabled` and `started` separately, any `refusal` class, listener, remote reach, APNs, mDNS, tailnet, device count, the gateway key fingerprint and the pairing in flight).

- Phone and host share no reachable LAN or tailnet address.
- The bind address or port is unavailable (the listener keeps retrying).
- The daemon was not restarted after a mobile config change.
- `bind` names one address the phone cannot reach: the pairing link and reconnects list only that address.
- APNs `topic` or `environment` does not match the app build.
- The APNs settings do not resolve (`FERMIX_APNS_KEY` unreadable behind a locked keychain, a key that is not a P-256 `.p8`, a missing or invalid field): the phone channel still starts, without push, the daemon log names the fault, and `fermix doctor` reports APNs credentials missing. Fix `[fermix_channels.mobile.push]` (or unlock the keychain) and restart.
- The phone was revoked or reinstalled: pair again.
- Memory is off (`[fermix_core.memory] enabled = false`): the phone channel keeps its timeline there, so it is refused for that boot as `memory_disabled` (every other channel keeps running).
- `devices.toml` is unreadable, corrupt, empty or not `0600`: the mobile surface refuses to start for that boot (every other channel keeps running). Repair or remove the file, then restart.

Turns keep the `main-*` trace session and group in Opik under the `mobile:main` thread.
