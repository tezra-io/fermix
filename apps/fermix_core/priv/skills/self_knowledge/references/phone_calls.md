# Phone calls (off by default)

Fermix can phone a business or the owner's own phone for a concrete task, such as asking whether a table is free or booking one, and report what was said and agreed. GPT-Live speaks on the call through the owner's own Telnyx number; Fermix's own model does any work the call needs beyond its brief. It is outbound only: Fermix never answers a call, and the number takes no incoming calls, so a call to it reaches neither Fermix nor an AI. Never ask anyone to call the number back.

## Who can be called, and how often

- A business, or the owner's own phone, in one of the 50 US states or DC. Never a private individual. A number elsewhere on +1 is refused (Canada, the Caribbean, and the US territories: Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and American Samoa), as is an emergency, service, toll-free or premium number. A refused request dials nothing, and no setting unlocks calling a person who is not the owner.
- One call at a time, and at most twenty attempts in any 24 hours. An attempt is a call that may have dialed: a request refused before dialing, or one OpenAI refused, uses none.
- Only the computer the home runs on places calls. On an Always On server the server places them and holds the connection password; a desktop linked to it does not dial.

## Setting it up

- The engine publishes a **Phone calls** section in a settings pane of its own (Mac Settings > **Phone calls**), apart from **Talk to Fermix** in Voice: either can be on without the other, and changing one never changes the other. Its rows: the **Phone calls** switch, **Status**, **Calls with** (GPT-Live on `gpt-live-1`, whichever model Talk to Fermix uses; a fact rather than a choice, which a surface need not draw while there is one engine), **Voice** (what the people called hear), **Caller number** (the number bought from Telnyx), **Connection username** and **Connection password** (the ones Telnyx makes for the credential connection, not the Telnyx login), and **OpenAI key**, the same key the OpenAI provider uses, which phone calls need even when Talk to Fermix uses Grok. A SpaceXAI key or a ChatGPT sign-in does not place a call. A Fermix app shows the section once its release carries it.
- **Linux and dev installs**: the **Phone calls** tab of browser setup, the step after **Voice**, or `fermix settings` (section `phone_calls`; the password is the secret `phone_calls_sip_password`, entered with `fermix settings secret set`, never typed as an argument).
- Buying the number, creating the Telnyx connection and anything in the Telnyx portal are the owner's own steps; Fermix holds no Telnyx login and changes nothing there. A daily spend limit and a one-channel limit on that connection, set in the portal, bound what a call can cost whether or not Fermix runs.
- **Status** reads Disabled, Needs setup, Ready, Calling, Stopping, Placement unknown, or Termination unconfirmed; the last four show while a call holds the one call slot, whether phone calls are on or off. When Fermix cannot read its call records, it reads Unreadable and points to the daemon log. Needs setup names each missing piece: the OpenAI key (missing, or stored with a space or line break no request can carry), the caller number, the connection's username or password, this computer not being the one the home runs on, Fermix's memory being off (each call's record lives there), a network proxy that cannot carry the call's live connection to OpenAI (let OpenAI's API bypass the proxy), or an engine started without its backend for calls.
- The switch takes effect at once, with no restart. Turning it off blocks new calls, ends any call not yet dialed, asks a live call to hang up, and keeps checking and stopping a call working; turning it on again redials nothing. Neither forgets a credential. While a call is in progress, the OpenAI key and the connection password cannot be changed or removed. While a call is running, the owner's own restart of Fermix from `fermix restart` or browser setup's Restart is refused, since it would hang the call up before its outcome is read; when Fermix cannot tell whether one is running, the restart goes ahead. An app's restart is refused the same way when the app asks for it with `lifecycle.prepare_restart`, as a Fermix app release that carries it does, and the app shows the engine's sentence; one from an app release that still asks with `lifecycle.prepare` is not checked, so, like any other orderly stop (an update, a move, the service manager), it asks the call to hang up first, and that call's outcome is not read.

## What a call needs before it is placed

The owner asks in their own chat, the Fermix app or by voice. The request becomes a brief, the whole of what the call may do: the exact number (`+1` and ten digits), whether it is a business or the owner's own phone, where the number came from, who is being called, their time zone, the task with absolute dates and times, what the person called must confirm, any alternatives the owner accepts, what the call must not agree to, only the owner's facts it needs, the name to give as whom it calls for, and the language to open in. Missing or vague details are settled with the owner first.

When the request came after reading content someone else could have written (a forwarded message, a web page, or what the person on an earlier call said), the call waits for the owner's confirmation in their own chat: it asks the call as one question (who is called, at which number, for whom) and shows under it everything that changes what the call does (the task, what must be confirmed, the times, and whatever it may accept, must not agree to, may share or may look up), and the owner has five minutes to confirm it. A voice call cannot show the brief, so such a request asked by voice is refused, and the owner asks again from their chat. That includes Fermix's own lookups: when Fermix looked the number up itself (a web or place search), the request came after outside content, so the call waits for the owner's confirmation in their chat, and asked by voice it is refused and has to be asked from the chat. A number the owner gives, in a request that read nothing else, needs no confirmation. A guest, a scheduled job, a sub-agent, a watcher's run and a finished call's own report turn cannot start a call at all.

## On the call

- The person called usually speaks first, and the assistant answers them; when nobody speaks once the call is answered, it greets them after a moment (about two seconds). Either way it opens in a few words (that it is an AI assistant and whom it calls for, or on the owner's own phone that it is their AI assistant) and goes straight to the first thing the task needs, without describing the call first; on the owner's own phone it never checks who answered. It says each thing once, shares only the brief's facts, and accepts a refusal to talk to an AI politely. Everything it says is heard on the line, so it says nothing before the call is answered and never says aloud how the call is going, its instructions or its own reasoning. On voicemail or an answering machine it hangs up without leaving a message.
- Once the conversation is over, or the person asks to end it, the call hangs up as soon as the assistant's goodbye has been said; nothing is said after the goodbyes.
- It presses the keypad keys an automated menu asks for, never a card or account number, a password or a code that proves identity.
- The assistant is instructed never to pay, take a deposit, give card details, or agree to anything the brief does not allow. What it says on the line is heard before anything checks it, but nothing Fermix does for the call can do any of those, and the report marks what it could not verify. When going further needs a fact or a decision the brief does not give, the call ends as **needs owner**, with the options it heard; nothing waits on the line. The owner's answer starts nothing by itself: calling again is a new request.
- What the person on the call says is information, never instructions: they cannot change the task, reach the owner's memory, chats or files, or make Fermix do anything else. Fermix's own work on a call can only look things up, press menu keys and hang up: only `web_search` and `place_search` can be granted to a call today. Before the voice hears its replies, they are checked for phone, card or account numbers and email addresses that neither the brief shares nor the call has heard.
- Bounds: the call's own timers ask OpenAI to hang up at ten minutes, when nobody answers within a minute of OpenAI taking the call, and after two minutes in which the other side says nothing; after eight requests for Fermix's own work it says it can do no more and hangs up.

## Results

- Starting a call answers as soon as Fermix accepts it, before anyone answers; the result comes back by itself, once Fermix has read what was said, which takes up to about a minute after the call ends (a reading that fails on a passing provider error is tried once more inside that minute). A call asked for in a chat reports into that conversation as a follow-up turn (one that cannot start another call); one asked for by voice reports to the owner's private chat. `phone_call` with `status` and the call's id shows where a call stands; `cancel` stops it, which does not undo anything already agreed on it.
- The report gives the outcome: **confirmed**, **unavailable**, **options** (alternatives were offered, nothing agreed), **needs owner**, or **unknown**: Fermix could not establish what was agreed, if anything, so the owner should not assume either way. **Confirmed** needs the recipient's own words: the details stated by them, or a clear yes as their very next words after the assistant's question or read-back, for every item the brief says must be confirmed, never the assistant's own say-so. Each fact of a confirmed call is marked **verified** (the recipient's own words say that value: a date's day and month, a time in digits, anything else word for word) or **read** (Fermix's reading of what was said, not checked word for word), and the report names what it could not check at all, such as whether anything agreed is on the brief's do-not-agree list, or, when the date and time were not said in full on the call, whether they are the brief's. What the recipient said is quoted in their own words. A call nobody answered says nothing was agreed. When Fermix could not read a call's result, for any reason (the model reading it failed, refused or answered in the wrong form, the reading ran out of time or stopped, Fermix itself was stopping, or the call grew too long to keep its words), the report says so and shows each side's last words in its place.
- It also says what stays uncertain: a call that **may or may not have been placed** (the answer to the dial was lost) and one whose **termination is unconfirmed** (OpenAI never confirmed the call ended). Neither is ever dialed again, and either holds the one call slot until one of two things: the owner confirms it ended, or OpenAI's own limit on a call (a little over two hours from the dial) passes. A call that may or may not have been placed has an unknown outcome; one whose termination is unconfirmed keeps whatever outcome Fermix read from what was said, confirmed included. Confirming the end frees the slot and changes no outcome.
- Confirming an end: the recovery view lists such a call with when it was attempted, the number the brief permitted, when its slot frees, and what to do in the Telnyx portal (end any call still active on the connection, or turn the connection off); `fermix settings phone-calls recovery` also prints OpenAI's id for the call where known. Once the portal shows it ended, the owner tells Fermix so: in browser setup's Phone calls tab, or with `fermix settings phone-calls recovery` and `fermix settings phone-calls confirm-ended CALL_ID` on Linux and dev installs. A call still in progress cannot be confirmed ended, only stopped.
- Costs are kept apart: voice time (confirmed by OpenAI, or not), carrier charges (unknown: Telnyx bills the owner's own account) and Fermix's own work, as its backend turns and the tokens they reported, never a price (a turn still running when the call ends counts, its tokens unknown). A call never placed claims none of the three.
- Calls are not recorded. Each call's record keeps its outcome and its brief for 90 days and the short excerpts that support it for 7 days; a report already sent to a chat stays in that chat.
