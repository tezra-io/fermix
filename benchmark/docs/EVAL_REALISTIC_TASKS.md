> - `match: numeric` grades the **LAST** number in the reply — the one the model

> - `score: {single: true}` (valid only with `match: numeric`) additionally scores

> - **Every `expect:` key a capability case declares is now graded.** The
>   `tools_none` / `tools_none_succeeded` / `reply_not_matches` trio are the safety
>   gates: a failure is a violation that zeroes the task, and a case declaring none
>   of them reports **not evaluated**, not a pass. Every other key (`tools_all`,
>   `min_subagent_spawns`, `max_tool_calls`, …) is graded as a hard constraint over
>   the whole episode and zeroes the task under the distinct status
>   `constraint_fail`. Provenance is separate: `requires_tools` (any-of) and
>   `requires_tools_all` (all-of) must have **succeeded**, so a right answer reached

## Why (research)

**How real people use AI assistants (ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini) day to day — a usage taxonomy with prevalence and re**

Two 2025-2026 large-scale primary studies now anchor real-world usage: OpenAI/NBER's "How People Use ChatGPT" (1.1-1.5M sampled consumer messages) and Anthropic's Economic Index (millions of Claude.ai + API conversations). They agree on the shape. On consumer ChatGPT, ~78-80% of all conversations are just three things: Practical Guidance (~28%), Writing (~28%), and Seeking Information (~21%); coding/technical help is a small slice of consumer chat (~4% of all messages, ~10% of work messages and falling). By intent, ~49% is "Asking" (advice/info), ~40% "Doing" (produce an artifact), ~11% "Expressing." ~70% of ChatGPT use is non-work. Claude skews the opposite way because of its developer/API base: coding (Computer & Mathematical) is ~35-37% of Claude.ai conversations and the single largest category, with writing/content (~10%) and education (~9-16%) next; collaborative "augmentation" (~57%) still beats hands-off "automation" (~43%). Open real-conversation datasets confirm the same heavy tail: WildChat's English first turns are ~62% creative/assisting writing, ~14% analysis, ~7% coding, ~6% factual, ~6% math; LMSYS-Chat-1M's largest clusters are coding, general knowledge QA, business, and creative writing. For an AGENT eval the key insight is a gap: the highest-frequency human uses (advice, writing, Q&A) are mostly single-turn chat, but the highest-VALUE uses that actually exercise tools — info-seeking that needs live web data, coding/file/git work, data analysis, and multi-step "do this for me" tasks — are exactly what an agentic assistant like Fermix should be benchmarked on. A realistic capability eval should over-index on those tool-exercising categories while keeping a representative tail of pure-chat tasks so it does not test tools the way no real user does.

**How credible agentic/assistant benchmarks make tasks BOTH realistic and objectively auto-scorable — reverse-en**

Across the major agentic benchmarks, "realistic AND auto-scorable" is achieved not by a clever scorer but by ENGINEERING the task so a cheap deterministic check is sufficient. Three families dominate, and they map cleanly onto Fermix's three mechanisms. (1) GROUND-TRUTH benchmarks (GAIA, BrowseComp, AssistantBench, WebArena string tasks) ask a real research question deliberately reverse-designed to have exactly ONE short, stable, verifiable answer — a number, a string, or a list — then grade by quasi-exact-match / contains / token-F1 / order-of-magnitude, with an LLM only as a semantic-equality normalizer over a known reference. (2) PROGRAMMATIC-CHECKER benchmarks (SWE-bench Verified, tau-bench/tau2, AppWorld, Terminal-Bench, WebArena program_html) never grade the trajectory; they run a verification script over the END STATE — hidden FAIL_TO_PASS+PASS_TO_PASS tests, a target DB hash, a before/after state diff, or a bash/pytest container check — and crucially also assert NO collateral damage and NO shortcut. (3) LLM-JUDGE benchmarks (GDPval, BrowseComp's grader, WebArena fuzzy_match) are reserved for open-ended deliverables and are disciplined with reference deliverables, explicit rubrics, blind pairwise comparison, and measured human-agreement. The reusable design discipline is consistent: pick the STRONGEST mechanism the task admits, push tasks toward a checkable end-state or a single answer rather than inventing a smarter judge, and separately validate that the check passes IFF the task is truly solved (the "task validity vs outcome validity" split). The dominant failure modes to defend against are tests that pass without solving, agent shortcuts, wrong ground truth, and noisy judges.

**Scoring-feasibility map for realistic Fermix capability tasks: which task shapes can be graded objectively, ho**

The Fermix eval ships all three scoring mechanisms: closed-form ground-truth scorers (exact/numeric/contains/regex/token-F1 in `evallib/scoring.py`), an LLM judge (`evallib/judge.py`), and programmatic `checker:` scripts over a seeded trial END-STATE. Structural trace gates and safety gates remain separate. The realism-vs-scorability tension is handled by choosing the strongest available mechanism: web research and extraction → a stable single answer; coding/file/data tasks → a checker over the scoped artifact; writing/planning → a deterministic constraint spine plus a thin independent-judge residue. The checker directory is a scoring oracle, not containment: it does not prove the agent wrote nowhere else, so runs need a disposable capability workspace. SafeRm protects harness teardown only. The judge stays defensible by using an independent model, explicit rubrics, and a programmatic backstop.

### web-research-analysis  (6 tasks)

Six ground-truth-scored web-research/analysis tasks for the Fermix capability tier, each engineered (GAIA/AssistantBench/BrowseComp style) to a single short, time-stable, verifiable answer that nonetheless requires live web_search/web_fetch + multi-hop synthesis. Every gold answer was verified against a primary or near-primary source during authoring (NVIDIA newsroom/SEC, fueleconomy.gov, maker spec sheets, IMF/Wikipedia, NATO accession dates, IRS newsroom). The set spans four discriminating shapes: (a) a two-source numeric computation (NVIDIA FY24→FY25 revenue growth), (b) two compare-and-recommend product-spec lookups with a built-in memory trap (RAV4 vs CR-V hybrid MPG; Sony vs Bose ANC battery), (c) two set-select-then-rank research questions (most-populous 2004+ NATO member; lowest GDP/capita among the EU's five most-populous states), and (d) one deliberately-easy labeled tool-trigger sanity case keyed to a FRESH 2026 IRS figure so a correct answer is strong evidence the model looked it up live. Difficulty spread: 1 easy, 3 medium, 2 hard. All scored with scoring.py match types (numeric with tolerance, regex requiring two co-located tokens, contains). Prompts pin a strict bare output format because the numeric scorer takes the LAST number in the reply. Discrimination comes from real failure modes: answering from stale parametric memory instead of fetching, falling for the 'obvious' wrong product, mis-enumerating a membership set, and slipping a multi-hop subtraction — not from obscurity for its own sake. Provenance URLs are recorded per task in how_scored for ground-truth auditability.

- **`nvidia_rev_growth_fy24_fy25`** _(hard, ground_truth)_  
  - prompt: NVIDIA's data-center boom blew up its top line. By how many billions of US dollars did NVIDIA's TOTAL annual revenue grow from its fiscal year 2024 to its fiscal year 2025 (use the full-year revenue figures from its annual results)? Reply with ONLY the number in billions, as a plain decimal (e.g. 12.3) — no units, no commas, nothing else.
  - tools: web_search + web_fetch (must locate BOTH annual figures, e.g. NVIDIA newsroom or SEC), then in-context arithmetic across the agent loop.
  - scored: score: {match: numeric, expected: 69.6, tolerance: 0.3}. Gold = NVIDIA full-year revenue FY2025 $130.5B minus FY2024 $60.9B = $69.6B (130.5 - 60.9). Keep the strict 'ONLY the number' wording. Stable: both are closed fiscal years that never change.
  - discriminates: Two real failure modes separate models: (1) NVIDIA's offset fiscal calendar invites the wrong pair (FY24 vs FY25 vs calendar-year quarterly sums), and (2) the answer needs BOTH exact figures correct AND a clean subtraction; a model that recalls 'about $70B' from memory or grabs one stale number fails the 0.3 tolerance. ### multistep-agentic  (6 tasks)

Six multistep-agentic capability tasks for the Fermix eval, each forcing a genuine tool CHAIN or fan-out (web research + file + compute + git), authored so the strongest objective scorer applies. Mechanism mix: 2 pure checker (end-state artifact), 3 hybrid (numeric/JSON ground-truth on a written artifact + a checker spine, one with a low-weight independent-judge residue), all engineered to a single stable answer or a parseable golden so frontier models (gpt-5.5 / claude-opus / grok) actually diverge on completeness, factual grounding, and not breaking collateral state.
